## Get emergency help for these signs

Call 911 or go to the emergency room right now if you or the person you care
for has any of these:

- A first seizure, or a seizure that will not stop
- Sudden weakness, numbness, or trouble speaking
- A sudden severe headache, or a headache that keeps getting worse
- Throwing up over and over
- Confusion, or trouble staying awake

These need care today, not an appointment next week.

## If none of those are happening, take a breath

Most people who are newly diagnosed have days or weeks to plan, not hours.
You do not have to understand all of this today. The people who care for you
have time to explain things more than once.

## What helps in the first week

- **Write down your exact diagnosis.** Ask your team to spell it. The words
  on your pathology report shape which treatments and trials fit you.
- **Bring someone to appointments.** Two people hear more than one. It is
  fine to record the visit if you ask first.
- **Write your questions before you go.** You will forget them in the room.
  Everyone does.
- **Ask who your point of contact is.** Most teams have a nurse navigator.
  That person is your fastest answer to most questions.

## Questions worth asking your team

- What type and grade is my tumor?
- Was any gene testing done, and what did it show?
- What are my treatment choices, and what happens if I wait?
- Is there a clinical trial that fits me?
- Who do I call when something changes?

## Where to talk to a person

The American Brain Tumor Association CareLine is free, and the people who
answer know brain tumors. It is open on weekdays during business hours.

Call [800-886-2282](tel:8008862282).

Nights and weekends, or if you are in crisis, see
[get help now](/get-help-now) for lines that answer any hour.

## What to do next

Write down your exact diagnosis and three questions for your next visit. Then
call the CareLine if you want to talk it through with a person.
