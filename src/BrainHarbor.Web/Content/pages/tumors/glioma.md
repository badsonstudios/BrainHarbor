## What a glioma is

Your brain has nerve cells, and it has helper cells around them. The helper
cells are called glial cells. A glioma is a tumor that starts in those helper
cells.

Glioma is a group, not one thing. Doctors sort gliomas by which helper cell
they came from and how fast they grow.

## Why the word matters

If your report says glioma, look for the more exact word next to it. It may say
astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma, ependymoma, or glioblastoma. That word tells you
more than glioma alone.

Your report may also give a grade, from 1 to 4. A lower grade grows more slowly.

## What to ask your team

- Which kind of glioma is mine?
- What grade is it?
- Where in my brain is it?
