Almost everybody asks this, usually in the first week, usually in private. It is
worth answering plainly.

**For most brain tumors, nobody knows the cause.** That is the honest state of
it. The exact cause of most brain and spinal cord tumors is not fully
understood, and there are very few well established risk factors. Tumors start
when genes that control how cells grow and divide are changed, and in most cases
those changes happen **by chance** during a person's life. Not because of
something done or not done.

**Cell phones.** They give off a kind of radiation called radiofrequency. It is
not the same as the radiation in an x-ray. The energy is too low to damage DNA,
and it is not known to cause cancer. The evidence so far is that using a cell
phone does not cause brain tumors.

**Stress.** There is no scientific evidence that stress causes brain tumors, and
no data linking stress to a higher chance of getting one. A hard year before a
diagnosis is a hard year. It is not a cause.

**Something you ate, or did not eat.** No food and no diet is an established
cause of a brain tumor. It is not on the list of known risk factors at all.

**Something you passed on, or caught.** Brain tumors are not contagious. Most
are not inherited either. A small number of people do have an inherited
condition that raises the risk, and if that applies to you, your team will raise
it and offer to talk it through.

If you are turning this over at three in the morning, that is ordinary and it
happens to nearly everyone. It is also worth saying out loud to somebody on your
team, because they have answered it many times and they would rather you asked.
