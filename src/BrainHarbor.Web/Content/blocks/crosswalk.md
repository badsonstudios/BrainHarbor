The rules for naming brain tumors changed in 2021. If your paperwork was written
before that, some of its words are no longer used.

**This matters more than it sounds.** People read an old report next to a new
one, see two different names, and think somebody made a mistake. Usually nobody
did. The tumor did not change. The way doctors write it down changed.

Three changes affect almost every report.

- **Roman numeral grades**, such as grade II or grade IV on an older report.
  These are now written with ordinary numbers instead: grade 2, grade 4.
  Nothing about the grade itself changed. Only the way of writing it did.
- **Gene results are now part of the name.** Older reports named a tumor mostly
  by how the cells looked. A name written now usually has a gene result built
  into it. That is why a newer report can look longer and more technical than
  an older one about the same tumor.
**You may also see NOS or NEC** written next to your diagnosis. These say
something about the testing, not about how serious the tumor is. NOS means the
tests needed to be more exact were not available. NEC means testing was done,
but the results did not fit any defined group neatly.

Seeing an older name on your own report does not mean it was wrong. It means it
was written under the old rules. If you are holding an old report and a new one
and they do not match, that is a good thing to take to your team and ask about.
