Most people are told what they have long before anyone explains what it is
doing. These are the ways a tumor in the brain causes symptoms. They overlap,
and most people have more than one going on at once. Which of them apply to you
depends on what your tumor is and where it sits.

**It takes up room.** The skull is a closed box. It cannot stretch. So anything
new inside it has to push on something. The tumor and any swelling around it
together take up space and press on nearby parts of the brain.

**It can make the brain around it swell.** This one surprises people, and it is
worth understanding. Fluid builds up in the brain tissue near the tumor. That
swelling plays a major part in deciding what symptoms you get. Some of what you
are feeling may not be the tumor itself. It may be the swelling around it. That
is why medicines that bring swelling down can change how somebody feels, without
the tumor being any different than it was.

**Pressure can build up inside the head.** When the pressure inside the skull
rises, there is a pattern doctors watch for: headaches that are worse in the
morning, feeling sick and throwing up, odd eye movements, seizures, and being
confused or very drowsy. Not everybody with raised pressure has all of it, and
some people have almost none of it. If this pattern is new for you, it is worth
a phone call rather than a wait.

**It can set off seizures.** A tumor and the irritated brain around it can upset
the normal electrical activity of that part of the brain. For some people a
seizure is the first sign that anything is wrong.

**It can block the flow of fluid.** The brain makes a fluid that circulates
around it and drains away. A tumor sitting in the wrong place can block that
flow, so the fluid backs up and the pressure goes up. This is more likely with
tumors near the narrow channels the fluid passes through. It can be treated, and
the usual treatment is a **shunt**. That is a thin tube that carries the fluid
to another part of the body, where it is absorbed. It is the word your team will
use.

## Why your symptoms are the ones you have

The short rule: **the symptom tells you where, the scan tells you what.** A
tumor disturbs whatever job the brain does in that spot, so where it sits
decides what you notice.

- **Front of the brain.** Personality, behavior, planning, motivation, and on
  one side, producing speech.
- **Side of the brain, near the temple.** Seizures, memory, and understanding
  what people say.
- **Upper back part of the brain.** Sensation, knowing where your body is in
  space, reading, writing and arithmetic.
- **Back of the brain.** Vision, and losing part of your field of view.
- **Cerebellum, low at the back.** Balance, coordination and walking.
- **Brainstem.** Double vision, facial weakness, and trouble swallowing.

If something on this list matches what you have noticed, it is worth writing
down and taking to your next appointment. Your team can tell you whether it fits
where your tumor actually is.
