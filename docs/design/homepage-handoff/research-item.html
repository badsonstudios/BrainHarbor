A pill for a common gene change slowed low-grade glioma growth

Original title: “Vorasidenib in IDH1- or IDH2-Mutant Low-Grade Glioma (INDIGO): a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial”

The short version

  • Researchers tested a daily pill in 331 people with grade 2 that had an .
  • People who took the pill went much longer before their tumor grew — about 27 months compared to 11 months without it.
  • It also delayed the need for chemotherapy or radiation.
  • Side effects were mostly mild; some people had liver test changes that needed monitoring.
i

What this means — and doesn't mean

What it means: for people whose tumor has an IDH gene change, this pill may add years before stronger treatments are needed. It was tested in a large, careful study in people — the strongest kind of evidence.

What it doesn't mean: this is not a cure, and it does not apply to every brain tumor. It was only tested in grade 2 gliomas with an IDH change. Whether your tumor has this change is something your care team can tell you from your pathology report.

How early is this?
